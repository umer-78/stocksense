Stocky replacement guide: moving off Shopify Stocky

Updated October 2026 · 6 minute read

Shopify discontinued Stocky in August 2026. For most stores the painful loss is not a screen or a report, it is the weekly answer to one question: what do I need to reorder, and how much? This guide covers what to keep, where each of Stocky's jobs goes now, and how to get reorder suggestions running again.

1. Keep whatever Stocky data you still have

If you exported your data before the shutdown, you likely have two CSV files:

  • Stocky - Variants.csv: on-hand, available, committed and incoming quantities per variant, plus cost and lead time where you had set them.
  • Stocky - Purchase Orders.csv: your purchase-order history, with supplier, SKU, quantity ordered and received, cost and order date.

Store both somewhere safe. The purchase-order file is the most valuable: it records how long each supplier actually took and how much you ordered, which is exactly what you need to set lead times in any replacement.

No exports? You can still start. Forecasting runs on your Shopify order history and current inventory, and Shopify still holds both. Stocky's files are useful reference, not a requirement.

2. Write down the settings you relied on

Before you set anything up, note these per supplier (or per product, if they differ a lot):

  • Lead time: days from placing an order to stock being sellable.
  • Safety buffer: how much extra you keep for slow deliveries or sales spikes.
  • Cover: how many days of stock one order should last, which usually matches how often you reorder.

3. Decide where each of Stocky's jobs goes

Stocky did several jobs. No single app has to replace all of them, and most stores only used one or two.

Job Stocky didWhere it can go now
Forecasting and reorder suggestionsAn inventory forecasting app such as Forelark, which shows days to stockout, reorder point and suggested quantity per variant.
Building purchase ordersForelark's purchase-order builder exports a CSV you send to your supplier. Or a spreadsheet.
Supplier recordsA spreadsheet, or a full inventory-management app if you have many suppliers.
Receiving, adjustments, transfers, stocktakesShopify admin's own inventory tools, or a full inventory-management app.

4. Set up forecasting again

  1. Install the app and pick a location. Forecast all locations combined or a single one; you can switch at any time.
  2. Enter the settings from step 2. Defaults are a 14-day lead time, 20% safety stock, 30 days of cover and a 60-day sales lookback, so it works before you change anything.
  3. Import your Stocky CSVs (optional). Both files are accepted and stored as reference history.
  4. Check the dashboard. Variants are ranked critical (will run out before a new order could arrive), low (within twice your lead time) or healthy.

5. Run a weekly reorder routine

  1. Open the dashboard and start with the critical items.
  2. Read the explanation on any number that surprises you. Each one shows the sales, lead time and buffer behind it.
  3. Add what needs ordering to the purchase-order builder, adjust quantities, and export the CSV.
  4. Send it to your supplier. Nothing is ordered automatically.

Want to sanity-check the math first? Run your own numbers through the free reorder point calculator. It uses the app's reorder formulas with a simple average; the app itself picks the best-tested forecast for each product.

Common questions

Can I still export my data from Stocky?

Stocky was discontinued in August 2026, so new exports may no longer be possible. If you saved the exports before the shutdown, keep them.

Do I need my Stocky data to start forecasting again?

No. Forecasts come from your Shopify order history and current inventory.

Does Forelark manage suppliers like Stocky did?

No. It focuses on forecasting and reorder quantities, and exports purchase orders as CSV. Keep supplier details in a spreadsheet or a dedicated inventory-management app.

Will it change my stock levels?

No. Forelark only reads inventory and orders. It never writes to Shopify.
